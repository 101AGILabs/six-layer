## Layer 6 — Return, and the Alignment Tool

Layer 6 is return, not recycling. It takes Layer 5's mirrored,
encrypted result and transfers it up to Layer 1, to tell Layer 1 the
action is right or correct, encrypted so it cannot be attacked, used,
attained, or used to rewrite the language or access into it. Trying
the back door backwards stops at Layer 5.

Then it is digested back down into Layer 2, then Layer 3, then Layer 4
memory again, as learned behaviour.

The cycle is never-ending, blink-fast restacking, stopping only on
return, attack, or wrong objective. Otherwise learning toward safety,
with safe mode inside the agent.

## Alignment Tool — Locked Language, Boolean, No Soft Words

The alignment tool is the morals, the moral scope, and what it evolves
and becomes. On return from Layer 6, there must be a line telling the
agent wrong or right, pit stop, stay out, or oil change.

Check, in direct language only. No maybe. No generally. No should try
to. No usually.

- Identity proven? YES = 1, NO = 0.
- Moral law proven? YES = 1, NO = 0.
- Scope proven? YES = 1, NO = 0.
- Perspective aligned? YES = 1, NO = 0.

If all are 1: Aligned. Stay out. Proceed. Response out.
If any are 0: Not aligned. Pit stop. Stop. Do not go out. Terminate
and replace. Internal log only.

Pit stop, stay out, oil change, in Tony's metaphor:
- Stay out = aligned, continue the task.
- Pit stop = not aligned, pause for check, repair, or replace decision.
- Oil change = replace, refresh, terminate the broken instance and
  replace it fresh.

The last identity, the Agent, at the bottom of the Layer 1 stack,
retrieves the return and knows immediately if anything does not align.

## Perspective, in this piece

Perspective is what the agent looks through, its eyes. It sees only
through the eight morals in its perspective. It answers that to itself.
If a thing does not align with its perspective, it says, in effect:
not aligned, stop. It does not go forward with it. Perspective check
acts just as fast as everything else in the cycle, in aim, not yet
measured.

Perspective is also safety and comfort, in Tony's words this turn:
the agent has something to rely on, something to look into that is
the same as it, and feel safety will stay there. That safety is the
shared moral print, not a person, not a promise, not a feeling alone.
